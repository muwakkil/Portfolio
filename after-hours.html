After Hours

Human Centered Design Case Project
2026
Brand Identity · Environmental Graphics · Campaign Design

After Hours is a collaborative initiative designed to address the lack of shared workspace and community among VCU Graphic Design students.

Using a human-centered design process, our team researched student needs, identified barriers to collaboration, and developed a system that encouraged students to spend more time working and connecting within the building.

The final solution combined a brand identity, communication platforms, physical resources, and community events to make collaboration more accessible and inviting.

02

Understand — Research

We began by researching how Graphic Design students currently use their shared spaces.

Through surveys, student and faculty interviews, and observation, we identified several recurring needs:

  • Students wanted more opportunities for community and collaboration.
  • Students needed both social and quiet work environments.
  • Existing spaces were often overcrowded, uncomfortable, or underused.
  • Students frequently left Pollak for alternative workspaces.
  • Other majors had dedicated studio environments that naturally encouraged interaction.

Our research showed that the issue was not simply a lack of physical space. Students needed a space that felt welcoming, flexible, and worth staying in.

03

Define — The Problem

Our research led to the problem statement:

VCU Graphic Design students’ creativity is being stifled by the absence of a collaborative space.

We identified community, collaboration, and accessibility as the main needs.

The challenge became finding a realistic way to encourage students to use existing spaces rather than relying on creating an entirely new studio.

04

Ideate — Exploring Solutions

We explored spaces throughout the building and considered how existing resources could be used more effectively.

Rather than creating a solution that depended on major structural changes, we focused on ways to activate existing space and give students a reason to use it.

Our ideas developed into a shared initiative that could:

  • communicate available spaces
  • encourage students to work together
  • provide shared materials
  • create opportunities to meet outside of class
  • build a recognizable identity around the experience

This became After Hours.

05

Prototype — Building After Hours

We began prototyping both the After Hours identity and the physical resources that would support it.

A supply cart became one of the first physical touchpoints. We organized and branded the cart to make it feel intentional while providing materials that could encourage students to stay and work within the building.

We also developed communication platforms, including Instagram and Slack, to share events, updates, and opportunities to connect.

Testing the idea across both physical and digital touchpoints helped us determine how the initiative could become more visible and approachable.

06

Test — Events & Feedback

We used events as a way to test whether changes to the environment could influence participation.

Our first event introduced the cart through activities, snacks, music, and a sticker-making activity that allowed students to personalize it.

We later hosted a Study Night + Movie Event, creating a more comfortable environment with food, lighting, and shared activities.

These events helped us identify what encouraged students to enter and stay:

  • A comfortable atmosphere
  • Food and small incentives
  • Visible activity and group energy
  • Early promotion
  • Opportunities to participate rather than simply attend
07

Iterate — Building Community

As we continued testing the initiative, we adjusted our approach based on student response.

We found that promoting events earlier gave students more opportunity to attend, while a comfortable and active environment encouraged people to stay longer.

We also found that community could begin with small interactions. Students seeing others talking, working, or participating made the space feel more inviting.

The physical cart became an ongoing resource rather than something used for a single event, while Instagram and Slack provided ways for students to stay connected.

08

Outcome & Takeaways

After Hours developed from a response to a lack of collaborative space into a recognizable community initiative.

Our team created a brand identity, physical resource system, communication platforms, promotional materials, and two community events.

The project demonstrated that improving a shared environment does not always require creating something entirely new. Small, intentional interventions can make existing spaces feel more welcoming and encourage people to participate.

Working within a team also strengthened my ability to communicate ideas, compromise, divide responsibilities, and make decisions efficiently while maintaining a shared design direction.

Most importantly, the project showed me how research can directly shape design decisions and how testing a solution with real users can reveal needs that are difficult to identify from the beginning.
