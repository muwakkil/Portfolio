Speaking in Motifs

Capstone Project
2026
UX/UI · Web Design · Interactive Installation
View Site ↗

Speaking in Motifs is an interactive design experience that explores Syrian and Islamic architectural motifs as a visual language. Through a website, physical installation, and takeaway, the project translates cultural research into an experience where visitors can learn, interact, and create their own interpretations.

Developed around preservation, accessibility, and participation, the project explores how cultural knowledge can move beyond documentation and become something people actively engage with and carry forward. The final experience brought together research, UI/UX, visual systems, web development, and physical design across multiple touchpoints.

02

Starting Point

My interest in cultural motifs began through my connection to the architecture and visual environments I grew up around. Over time, I became interested in how the physical designs used in architecture were shaped not only by aesthetics and symbolism, but also by climate, geometry, function, and construction.

An earlier project, Stitched Stories, introduced me to combining education with participation. Seeing how the project sparked curiosity and encouraged people to interact with the subject motivated me to develop that idea further through Speaking in Motifs.

03

Research & Discovery

My research began broadly across Syrian visual culture and design, rather than immediately narrowing myself to one type of motif. I explored different forms of visual systems to understand what could meaningfully inform the project.

As the research developed, I found that architectural motifs provided the strongest foundation. Their historical and structural qualities allowed me to explore not only what a motif looks like, but why it was designed that way and how its underlying systems could be translated into an interactive experience.

I researched Syrian architecture, symbolic geometry, Islamic visual systems, and the relationship between mathematics, symbolism, environment, and function.

This research also began shaping the visual direction of the project. Typography, color, layout, and motif development were explored alongside the research rather than treated as separate decisions.

The motifs became more than decorative elements. Their geometry, repetition, and structure began informing the project's visual language and the way information could be organized within the interface.

This led to two guiding questions:

  • How can visual logic become interactive logic?
  • How can an archive encourage interpretation and participation?
04

Testing Participation

I tested the idea of learning through participation through a workshop exploring Aghabani, a textile tradition associated with Damascus.

Participants learned about the symbolism behind different motifs before using them to create their own interpretations. Although everyone began with the same information, every outcome was different.

This showed me that cultural education could become more engaging when users were given the opportunity to interpret and respond to what they learned rather than simply observe it.

The workshop became an early test for the type of participation I wanted to build into the final website.

05

Prototyping

With the research and visual direction established, I began prototyping how the website could turn the research into an actual user experience.

I created multiple prototypes and tested different systems for presenting information, guiding users, and connecting learning with interaction. One of my biggest challenges was determining how much information a user should receive, when they should receive it, and how they could interact with it without feeling overwhelmed or confused.

Peer feedback became an important part of this process. Testing helped me identify interactions that felt disconnected, information users were overlooking, and problems that were difficult for me to recognize on my own.

I used these observations to refine the UI, navigation, information hierarchy, and interactions so that the experience could guide users more naturally.

The goal was not only to make the interface functional, but to make the structure itself support the project's purpose: learning, interpreting, and creating.

06

Expanding Beyond the Screen

I extended the experience into physical space to create a complete journey.

The installation was designed to attract attention and create immersion. The website provided the space for learning, interaction, and preservation. The takeaway allowed visitors to continue engaging with the project after leaving.

Each component went through its own rounds of prototyping and refinement. It was important that the pieces felt connected rather than like separate deliverables.

The final system was designed so that the visual language, interaction, and physical experience reinforced one another across each touchpoint.

07

Outcome

Speaking in Motifs became an exploration of how cultural research can be translated into an interactive design experience.

The project pushed me to work across research, visual identity, UI/UX, web development, and physical production while maintaining cohesion between each component.

I evaluated the final experience through conversations with visitors and fellow students during the exhibition. The response showed that the installation was successfully drawing people into the experience, with visitors consistently interacting with the website. The takeaway pieces were also continually taken by visitors, requiring them to be replenished throughout the exhibition.

The website generated nearly 100 user-created pieces, demonstrating the level of participation I had hoped to encourage.

The project also exposed areas I would approach differently in a future iteration. I would refine some of the questions to provide clearer guidance, expand the archive, and improve how user-created work is stored and accessed across devices. During development, I initially struggled with saving content locally to individual devices, but ultimately implemented SPA routing to allow the experience to access saved images across devices.

This project reinforced the importance of prototyping, feedback, and iteration in understanding not only whether a product works, but how people actually experience it.
