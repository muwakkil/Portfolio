Speaking in Motifs

Capstone Project
2026
Website, Installation, Take-aways

Speaking in Motifs is an interactive design experience that explores Syrian and Islamic architectural motifs as a visual language. Through a website, physical installation, and takeaway, the project translates research into an experience where visitors can learn about cultural symbolism, interact with visual systems, and create their own interpretations.

Developed as an exploration of preservation, accessibility, and participation, the project considers how design can help cultural knowledge move beyond documentation and become something people actively engage with and carry forward.

02

Starting Point

My interest in cultural motifs began through my connection to the architecture and visual environments I grew up around. After moving back to the United States, I became increasingly interested in how design could help preserve and share cultural experiences that are not always easily accessible.

An earlier project, Stitched Stories, introduced me to the idea of combining education with participation. This became the starting point for Speaking in Motifs.

03

Research & Discovery

My research explored Syrian architecture, symbolic geometry, cultural preservation, and Islamic visual systems.

One challenge was the fragmented nature of available information. Rather than simply documenting motifs, I began asking how their underlying systems, structure, and logic could inform an interactive experience.

As the research developed, it also began shaping the project's visual direction. I explored typography, color, layout, and motif development to create a contemporary system rooted in architectural references without directly replicating them.

The motifs became more than decorative elements. Their geometry, repetition, and structure began informing the project's larger visual language and how information could be organized.

This led to two guiding questions:

  • How can visual logic become interactive logic?
  • How can an archive encourage interpretation and participation?
04

Testing Participation

I tested this idea through a workshop exploring Aghabani, a textile tradition associated with Damascus.

Participants learned about the symbolism behind different motifs before using them to create their own interpretations. Although everyone began with the same information, every outcome was different.

This reinforced the importance of participation and personal interpretation within the final experience.

05

Prototyping

With the research and visual direction established, I began prototyping how the website and different components of the experience would work together.

The website required the most iteration as I worked through its structure, navigation, information, and interactions. I tested different ways of organizing the research while making sure the experience remained accessible and engaging.

I really struggled with how the user would interact with the website and how I could truly influence the information they take in smoothly without overwhelming or confusing them.

A major challenge was balancing the amount of information with the interactive experience. I refined the structure and interactions to create a clearer relationship between learning and participation.

Prototyping also helped determine how the website, installation, and takeaway could function as parts of one cohesive experience rather than separate deliverables.

06

Expanding Beyond the Screen

I extended the experience into physical space to create a complete journey.

The installation was designed to attract attention and create immersion. The website provided the space for learning, interaction, and preservation. The takeaway allowed visitors to continue engaging with the project after leaving.

Together, these components created a connected experience across multiple touchpoints.

07

Outcome

Speaking in Motifs became an exploration of how research can be translated into an interactive design experience.

The project pushed me to work across research, visual identity, UX, web design, and physical production while maintaining cohesion between each component.

Most importantly, it reinforced my interest in designing systems that encourage people to move beyond observation and become active participants.
